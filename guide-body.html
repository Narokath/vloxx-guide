Fixate, Ascension and boon strip

Fixate: the purple diamond directs Vloxx’s targeted attacks. Call swaps immediately. Stealth, downstate or death can remove it. Weapon Aspects choose their own targets.

Ascension: start with ten stacks. Failed greens lose stacks and strengthen Vloxx. Collect weapon orbs to recover Ascension and reduce Empowered. Zero stacks is fatal.

Ordinary strips: the CM audit supports 11 − current Ascension boon types removed, favouring the newest applications or refreshes. Missing Ascension makes later hits more disruptive. Slice Through Reality can corrupt many boons at once.

Excision: landed hits refresh a roughly one-second strip lockout. Division can strip again much sooner; there is no universal strip-immunity window.

Weapon movement

Bring the Staff to Vloxx · bait early

  1. Send one baiter out immediately. An assigned player leaves early toward the planned first Annihilating Orb destination to get the Staff’s attention before the squad settles there.
  2. Give it a path into the new boss position. Position the baiter beyond the intended Vloxx stack from the Staff’s side, allowing for the Staff stopping once it can attack. The aim is to bring the Staff beside Vloxx at the new Orb destination.
  3. Keep Immobilize off the Staff during the setup. It can prevent the next approach from starting. Let any committed movement and current volley finish.
  4. Let the squad settle around the arriving Staff. The baiter rejoins once it is in position. Avoid a second distant player pulling its next approach elsewhere.

Start early rather than waiting for the whole squad to move. One designated baiter can establish the approach while the group handles Vloxx’s opening mechanics. If all players are already within the Staff’s attack range, it has no reason to move closer.

The Staff favours distant players, but a logged cast target is not a direct record of its navigation decision. Its stopping distance also matters: close-range attacks were observed around 240 units from their targets, with measured casts out to roughly 350 units. These are observations, not a confirmed fixed movement threshold.

Advanced · early Staff bait in recent pulls

16–18 October: 66 newly indexed recordings were checked; this comparison uses the 63 CM pulls and keeps three normal-mode recordings separate. Clem Fandango was the Staff’s first recorded player target in 23 pulls, at 1.01–3.53 seconds after Vloxx entered combat (median 2.36 seconds), before the first Annihilating Orb cast.

In 22 of those pulls, both the first Orb relocation and subsequent Staff positions were usable. The Staff came within 350 units of the new boss destination in 17, between arrival and 7.9 seconds afterward. That supports sending one player early to bring the Staff into the planned stack, rather than waiting for the whole group to move.

Examples: on 17 October at 16:42:44, Clem was targeted at +1.68 seconds and the Staff was already within 350 units when Vloxx relocated. On 18 October at 15:42:01, Clem was targeted at +2.36 seconds and the Staff was within 350 units 0.3 seconds after the relocation.

Method and limits: compare the Staff’s first cast with a player target in the first 15 seconds against the first Annihilating Orb cast, then identify Vloxx’s following position jump of more than 500 units. Measure Staff distance from that destination for the next 12 seconds. The initial targeted action is skill 80311; its target does not prove which player the movement AI sampled. Five tracked pulls did not meet the 350-unit proximity check, and one lacked subsequent Staff positions. This is evidence for the setup, not a controlled success rate or a guarantee. The recent sample is CM only.

What the deeper movement check found

Across 117 tracked CM approaches leading into Ancora Strike, the next target’s median distance fell from about 840 to 246 units. 116 closed the gap by more than 100 units.

Across 184 tracked LCM approaches leading into Ancora Strike, the next target’s median distance fell from about 748 to 238 units. 180 closed the gap by more than 100 units.

With at least eight living players tracked, Reflection chose the furthest player in 153 of 201 cases. All 393 measured Ancora casts were within about 351 units. The previous volley target matched the next strike target in only 15 of 107 approaches with a recent volley.

With at least eight living players tracked, Reflection chose the furthest player in 273 of 352 cases. All 578 measured Ancora casts were within about 353 units. The previous volley target matched the next strike target in only 10 of 170 approaches with a recent volley.

The volley’s distant-player preference is strong but not universal. Close-range attacks often choose a nearby player, with exceptions and summon targets. Use group movement rather than a guaranteed Staff baiter.

Staff movement under Immobilize

Finishing an approach and starting the next approach appear to behave differently. The Staff can keep travelling while Immobile is active. That does not establish that it can start a fresh approach while already immobilized.

The targeted check supports a movement-start restriction: existing movement can continue, while a stationary Staff may wait for Immobilize to end before moving again. This is a working explanation, not a confirmed rule about its internal target selection. The logs expose cast targets, not the AI’s navigation decisions.

Practical implication: Immobilize applied during travel may not stop the Staff coming in. Immobilize kept active while it is stationary or finishing a volley may delay its next approach. If the goal is to bring it into cleave, continuing to immobilize it before that approach could hold it at range. These timing recommendations still need a controlled comparison.

Advanced · movement continuation versus a new approach

Combined CM + LCM findings: all 408 challenge-mode recordings were processed together: 363 CM and 45 LCM, dated 16 September–15 October. Staff Immobilize applications appear in 43 of those 45 LCM recordings. Smaller counts below refer to selected observation windows, not the total recordings analysed. The 35 normal-mode recordings were also checked and are kept outside these pooled results.

Across CM and LCM, the Staff received 27,385 Immobilize applications, including 11,713 in P1. Existing travel continued after Immobilize became active in 397 qualifying intervals, including 163 in P1. No qualifying start from a recorded standstill had active Immobilize covering the surrounding test period.

Broad P1 observations: Immobilize was active at 865 volley endings. Four starts from a recorded standstill were detected within the following 1.2 seconds in this broader set, which includes cases where the condition did not cover the whole test period. Nearby players and subsequent casts also affect whether movement is needed; this broad count is not a controlled comparison.

Selected post-volley comparison: require uninterrupted condition coverage (or no Immobilize throughout), at least eight tracked living players, all tracked players at least 450 units away, and no following cast for 1.2 seconds. Touching or overlapping active stacks count as continuous coverage.

Why 43 out of 865? The filters exclude 631 for incomplete condition coverage, 178 with a player within 450 units, four with insufficient living-player tracking, and nine because another cast began. After Immobilize expires: in P1, 79 of 98 qualifying stationary cases resumed moving within 1.5 seconds. The median delay among those that resumed was about 0.52 seconds. Cases with immediate reapplication or an overlapping cast were excluded.

A P1 example: after the volley ended, two successive active Immobile stacks kept the condition present. The last removal was logged at +4.23 s; movement began at +4.66 s, about 0.42 s later. No intervening Staff cast was logged.

Interpretation: these pooled observations support a restriction on starting the next approach, while existing travel can continue. They do not prove whether the restriction is checked when choosing a navigation target, issuing a new path, or resuming after a cast. Cast targets are logged; internal navigation decisions are not. These findings concern Staff movement only.

Controlled check: keep destination, distance and summons constant. Compare Immobilize applied before the volley ends, applied only after walking starts, and no Immobilize. Record activation/removal, cast end, first movement and destination.

    Orbs and respawns

    The weapon bar opens at 25% health. Break it to release three Ascension orbs when Vloxx has Empowered stacks. At zero Empowered, no new weapon orbs are produced in the observed bar endings. Killing the weapon removes its immediate pressure. Weapons return about 40 seconds after death, so keep watching their spawn locations.
