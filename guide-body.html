Fixate, Ascension and boon strip

Fixate: the purple diamond directs Vloxx’s targeted attacks. Call swaps immediately. Stealth, downstate or death can remove it. Weapon Aspects choose their own targets.

Ascension: start with ten stacks. Failed greens lose stacks and strengthen Vloxx. Collect weapon orbs to recover Ascension and reduce Empowered. Zero stacks is fatal.

Ordinary strips: the CM audit supports 11 − current Ascension boon types removed, favouring the newest applications or refreshes. Missing Ascension makes later hits more disruptive. Slice Through Reality can corrupt many boons at once.

Excision: landed hits refresh a roughly one-second strip lockout. Division can strip again much sooner; there is no universal strip-immunity window.

How these timings were checked

All 509 indexed recordings were considered: 426 CM and 45 LCM recordings enter these comparisons; 38 normal-mode recordings are excluded. For Greens and Puddles, warning effects are identified by their content GUID in each log, with Vloxx as source. Marks within one second are grouped into a round, timed at its first recorded mark.

P4 starts at Vloxx’s Final Form channel, not the end of the preceding breakbar. The P4 Greens check contains 216 CM rounds from 92 recordings and 24 LCM rounds from eight recordings, giving 124 CM and 16 LCM repeat intervals. Puddles contains 218 CM rounds and 24 LCM rounds, giving 126 CM and 16 LCM intervals. Typical first marks are +3 seconds for Greens and +23 seconds for Puddles, measured from the channel start.

The P4 Greens median is 30.002 seconds in both modes; a first recorded mark can shift by about 0.08 seconds between targets, so the shortest measured gaps are just below 30 seconds. Some CM gaps are about 60 seconds because no intervening marker round was recorded. Puddles has a 20.000-second median in both modes. These observations do not prove the reason for a missing round.

For other mechanics, only Vloxx’s animation starts count. Each pair has the same actor, skill and health band, with 1–180 seconds between starts. Health bands label P1–P3 but do not identify every split boundary exactly. Interruptions and the rest of the rotation can affect gaps. A recording can contribute more than one interval.

The shared-mechanic warning checks span 16 September–18 October. Older detailed Staff and orb findings keep their original, separately stated sample sizes.

Spears

Raging Storm

CD window: ≥30.5 s between cast starts · observedCD window: ≥30.6 s between cast starts · observed

Plan for 20 Spears: four sequences of five launches, with three seconds between launches in the same sequence. They keep coming after Vloxx’s cast bar ends.

Do not return after the first impact. Tracked arrivals take about 2.5 seconds from projectile launch, with associated damage clustered around 2.5 and 3.0 seconds. The last launches occur around 17.4 seconds after the cast starts, so pressure can continue to roughly 20 seconds.

CM and LCM P2: keep the projectile-block rotation. Stack impact centres inside coverage; if coverage fails, leave the circles and the hazards they create.

Our log check · P2 Spears

All 426 CM and 45 LCM recordings were processed. Of casts with at least 22 seconds of remaining recording, 421 CM and 40 LCM casts had all 20 descending launches. Every one of these 461 casts formed four five-launch sequences. The median interval was 3.00 seconds; all measured within-sequence gaps in that complete set were 2.972–3.032 seconds.

Across all tracked P2 trajectories, there were 9,137 descending projectiles in CM and 841 in LCM. The median origin was 2,000 units above and 1,000 units horizontally from the recorded destination. Projectiles removed near their original destination, without a recorded relaunch, arrived at a median 2.518 seconds in both modes (1,080 CM and 67 LCM samples).

Positive hits associated by time and nearby player position cluster near 2.5 and 3.0 seconds after launch. Multiple overlapping Spears can make an individual association ambiguous. Projectile launches establish the timing reference; this audit does not establish the exact instant the visual warning circle appears. Partial casts and incomplete tracking can have fewer than 20 recorded launches.

Raging Storm

Wave every 3 s · measured

A new wave every three seconds. The common opening is four waves of three Spears, followed by two waves of six, then two waves of three. Recorded counts can be lower; keep following the actual circles rather than relying on a fixed count.

Our log check · P4 Spears

The full collection contained tracked P4 Spears in 92 CM and eight LCM recordings: 5,548 CM and 583 LCM descending projectiles. All 1,369 CM and 152 LCM measured wave intervals were within 0.06 seconds of three seconds; both medians were 3.00 seconds.

The most common CM opening was 3, 3, 3, 3, 6, 6, 3, 3 (61 recordings). The same pattern appeared in three LCM recordings; four other LCM recordings began with 2, 2, 2, 2, 4, 4, 2, 2. Reduced counts alone do not establish why targets were missing.

There were 332 recorded trajectory relaunches in CM and none in the LCM sample. A relaunch is not a general detector for every block or destroy, so this comparison is descriptive rather than a controlled field test. Near-destination arrivals took a median 2.520 seconds in CM and 2.522 seconds in LCM.

Keep falling-spear impact centres inside the assigned projectile field. If the field is missing or a spear lands, leave its circle and the hazard it creates.

Weapon movement

Bring the Staff to Vloxx

  1. Send one baiter early. Move toward the planned first Orb destination as the fight starts. Stand beyond the intended boss stack from the Staff’s side, so its approach brings it beside Vloxx.
  2. Keep Immobilize off until it arrives. Leave its route clear and let the current cast finish. Immobilize can prevent its next approach from starting.
  3. Rejoin once it is in position. Stack and cleave beside Vloxx. Avoid a distant straggler drawing the next approach elsewhere.

Give it a reason to move. If everyone is already within its attack range, the Staff stays put. It favours distant players, but cast targets do not prove exactly how its movement target is chosen. Allow for the Staff stopping short of the baiter once it can attack.

Advanced · early Staff bait in recent pulls

16–18 October: 66 newly indexed recordings were checked; this comparison uses the 63 CM pulls and keeps three normal-mode recordings separate. The assigned baiter was the Staff’s first recorded player target in 23 pulls, at 1.01–3.53 seconds after Vloxx entered combat (median 2.36 seconds), before the first Annihilating Orb cast.

In 22 of those pulls, both the first Orb relocation and subsequent Staff positions were usable. The Staff came within 350 units of the new boss destination in 17, between arrival and 7.9 seconds afterward. That supports sending one player early to bring the Staff into the planned stack, rather than waiting for the whole group to move.

Examples: on 17 October at 16:42:44, the baiter was targeted at +1.68 seconds and the Staff was already within 350 units when Vloxx relocated. On 18 October at 15:42:01, the baiter was targeted at +2.36 seconds and the Staff was within 350 units 0.3 seconds after the relocation.

Method and limits: compare the Staff’s first cast with a player target in the first 15 seconds against the first Annihilating Orb cast, then identify Vloxx’s following position jump of more than 500 units. Measure Staff distance from that destination for the next 12 seconds. The initial targeted action is skill 80311; its target does not prove which player the movement AI sampled. Five tracked pulls did not meet the 350-unit proximity check, and one lacked subsequent Staff positions. This is evidence for the setup, not a controlled success rate or a guarantee. The recent sample is CM only.

With at least eight living players tracked, Reflection chose the furthest player in 273 of 352 cases. All 578 measured Ancora casts were within about 353 units. The previous volley target matched the next strike target in only 10 of 170 approaches with a recent volley.

The volley’s distant-player preference is strong but not universal. Close-range attacks often choose a nearby player, with exceptions and summon targets. Use group movement rather than a guaranteed Staff baiter.

A P1 example: after the volley ended, two successive active Immobile stacks kept the condition present. The last removal was logged at +4.23 s; movement began at +4.66 s, about 0.42 s later. No intervening Staff cast was logged.

Interpretation: these pooled observations support a restriction on starting the next approach, while existing travel can continue. They do not prove whether the restriction is checked when choosing a navigation target, issuing a new path, or resuming after a cast. Cast targets are logged; internal navigation decisions are not. These findings concern Staff movement only.

    Coverage and method · 16 September–15 October: the earlier orb audit processed all 443 then-indexed Vloxx recordings were processed, including 363 CM and 45 LCM. Weapon orb missile creation (skill 80520) was checked from 0.5 seconds before to 1.5 seconds after the active bar ended, with at least 1.8 seconds of recording remaining.
